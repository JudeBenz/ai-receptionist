# Follow-up emails and texts

Send from your business email, plain text, no images, no HTML signature wider than four lines. Replace every bracket. Keep subject lines under seven words.

Signature to use everywhere:

```
[Your name]
AfterRing · AI receptionists for [HVAC, plumbing and legal] in [City]
[phone] · [email] · [website]
Hear it answer: call [demo number]
```

## Email 1: demo booked (send within five minutes of the call)

Subject: Your AfterRing demo, [Thursday 10:00]

Hi [First name],

Thanks for the time just now. You're booked for [Thursday at 10:00] (calendar invite attached).

Here's how the fifteen minutes go:

1. You call [demo number] from your own phone and pretend to be a customer. Ask for a price, change your mind, ask if it's a robot. Try to break it.
2. I show you what lands on the calendar and the summary that would go to your phone.
3. If it's useful, we talk about a 30-day pilot on your existing number. If not, no hard feelings.

One thing you said stuck with me: about [X] calls a day going to voicemail at roughly [$Y] a job. That's the number I'd like to show you a dent in.

Talk Thursday,
[signature]

## Text message templates (only after they've given you their mobile)

Demo reminder, the morning of:
> Hi [First name], [Your name] from AfterRing. Still good for [10:00] today? The number to call is [demo number]. Reply if you need to move it.

After "send me info":
> Hi [First name], [Your name] here, just sent the one-pager to [email]. Demo line is [demo number] if you've got two minutes.

Never text a business number that hasn't texted you first, and never send marketing texts to a mobile without explicit permission. Texting from a business brand at scale needs carrier registration (10DLC); person-to-person texts from your own phone to someone who gave you their number do not.
