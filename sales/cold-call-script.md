# Cold-call script and 100-call day plan

Goal for today: not to sell. Today you find out which owners miss calls and book them a 15-minute demo later this week, once the demo number is live. Whitney Bonds' rule applies cold as well as warm: you are asking for help with research, and you stop talking after each question.

Numbers to expect from 100 manual dials to business lines, from the sales reps' usual rule of thumb rather than anything in the videos: 30 to 45 people picked up, 12 to 20 real conversations, 3 to 6 demos booked. If you book three, the day worked.

## Before the first dial

- Lead list open (`lead-list-template.csv`), tracker open (`call-tracker.csv`).
- Calendar open so you can book demo slots on the spot.
- Your demo-booking email ready to send (`follow-up-emails.md`, email 1).
- Stand up. Smile. Use their business name in the first sentence.
- Dial by hand. No autodialer, no recorded message, no AI calling for you. Business-to-business calls to a company's published line, made by a person, are the low-risk kind. If anyone says "take us off your list", write DNC in the tracker and never call again.

## The opener (15 seconds)

> Hi, is this [Business name]? Great. Is the owner around, or whoever runs the office?

If they ask what it's about:

> I'm [Name], I'm local here in [City]. I'm not selling anything on this call, I'm doing some research on how [HVAC] companies handle their phones and I'm hoping to ask whoever runs things two quick questions. Would that be you?

When the owner or manager is on:

> Thanks for picking up. I'll keep this to sixty seconds. I'm building a service for [HVAC] companies around here and people keep telling me it would help, but I honestly don't know yet. Can I ask you two questions?

Then stop and wait for a yes.

## The two questions, then the third

> When the phone rings and everyone's out on a job, what happens to that call?

Listen. Write down exactly what they say. Then:

> Roughly how many calls a day would you say go to voicemail or just ring out?

Listen. Write the number. Then:

> And when someone does reach you, what's an average job worth, ballpark?

Write it. Do not do the multiplication out loud. Instead:

> Of the ones that go to voicemail, how many do you reckon would have booked if someone had just picked up?

Let them say it. This is the moment they are selling themselves.

## The pivot (only if they said they miss calls)

> That's really useful. So here's what I'm building: it's a phone receptionist that only answers when your people don't, after about fifteen seconds or after hours. It takes the details, books the job on your calendar, and texts you a summary. It tells people it's an AI, and anything it can't handle it hands to you.
>
> I'm doing fifteen-minute demos this week where you call the number yourself and try to confuse it. Would Thursday at ten or Friday at two be better?

Book it. Confirm their mobile number and email for the invite. Say:

> I'll send a calendar invite in the next five minutes with the number to call. If it ever gets in the way, just reply to the invite.

Hang up. Send email 1 immediately.

## If they say they don't miss calls

> That's good to hear, honestly, most people say they do. Last question: do you know anyone, a plumber, an electrician, a lawyer, who you think does miss calls? I'd love an intro.

Write the referral down. Thank them. Hang up.

## Gatekeeper script

> I understand. Could you tell me when [Owner] is usually at the desk, so I'm not interrupting? And could I get their first name so I'm not just saying "the owner"?

If they won't give it:

> No problem. Could you tell them [Name] from AfterRing called about missed calls after hours, and I'll try again [day]?

## Voicemail (under 20 seconds, once per business)

> Hi, this is [Name] in [City]. I'm doing a bit of research on how [HVAC] companies handle calls when everyone's on a job, two quick questions, nothing to buy. My number is [phone], that's [phone again]. Thanks.

Do not leave a second voicemail. Try once more at a different time of day, then move the lead to email.

## Objections

**"Not interested."**
> Fair enough, I appreciate you saying so. One question before I go: is that because you don't miss calls, or because you've heard the AI pitch before?
If the first, ask for a referral. If the second: "Understood. Mine only picks up when yours can't, and I'd rather you heard it than took my word for it. Fifteen minutes, you call it and try to break it. Thursday or Friday?"

**"We have a receptionist."**
> Good, keep them. This isn't for the calls they answer, it's for the ones at lunch, after six, and on Saturday when the furnace goes out. What happens to those right now?

**"We use an answering service."**
> How's it working out? ... What do they charge when you go over your minutes? (Most run two to five dollars a minute over the bundle.) This is a flat monthly number, and it actually books the job on your calendar instead of taking a message.

**"Customers hate talking to robots."**
> Agreed, which is why it says it's an AI up front and hands over the second someone asks for a person. The alternative tonight isn't a human, it's voicemail, and three out of four callers don't leave one.

**"How much is it?"**
> Flat monthly plans from about three hundred, and a 30-day pilot where you owe nothing if it hasn't booked you a job. But I'd rather you heard it first, because the price only makes sense after you've called it. Thursday or Friday?

**"Send me some information."**
> Happy to. What's the best email? ... I'll send a one-page summary now. What I'd suggest is you call the demo number on it when you get a minute, and I'll follow up Friday to see what you thought. Fair?

**"It's not our busy season."**
> That's actually the best time to set it up, before the phones go mad. And the pilot is month to month, so you can run it in the slow month and judge it in the busy one.

**"Is this a robocall?"**
> No, it's just me, [Name], dialing by hand from [City]. Bad timing?

**"Call me back later."**
> Of course. Morning or afternoon better? ... I'll put you down for [day, time]. If something comes up, no hard feelings.
Write the time in the tracker and keep it.

## The day plan

| Block | Dials | Notes |
|-------|-------|-------|
| 8:00 to 9:30 | 25 | Owners are at the desk before the trucks roll. Best block of the day. |
| 10:00 to 11:30 | 25 | Gatekeepers now. Ask for the owner's name and best time. |
| 12:00 to 12:30 | 0 | Send follow-up emails for the morning's conversations. |
| 1:00 to 2:30 | 25 | Second attempts on morning no-answers, then fresh leads. |
| 3:00 to 4:30 | 25 | Law firms answer the phone all day; put most of them here. |
| 4:30 to 5:00 | 0 | Tracker tidy-up, tomorrow's call-backs into the calendar. |

Keep every call under four minutes unless they are booking. If you are past four minutes and no demo is booked, ask for the demo or the referral and get off.

## After the day

Count: dials, conversations, "misses calls: yes", demos booked, referrals. Put the numbers in `LEARNINGS.md`. The three numbers owners gave you (missed calls a day, average job, would-have-booked) are your real pitch data from now on, better than any study.
